Solar Mounting Structure Support Spacing: Why Purlin Span Matters

V
By Velmurugan Rathinam
October 11, 2026
We are using the same profile and thickness. Why do we need another structural check?

Because the distance between supports has changed.

When evaluating solar mounting structures, we often compare material grade, section dimensions and steel thickness.

But another dimension deserves equal attention:

The unsupported span—the distance a member bridges between its supports.

A profile verified for one support spacing is not automatically suitable for a longer span.

A Simple Example: 2 Metres vs 3 Metres

Consider two illustrative purlin arrangements:

  • Arrangement A — support spacing: 2 metres
  • Arrangement B — support spacing: 3 metres

For this example, both have:

  • The same steel profile and thickness.
  • The same material properties and section orientation.
  • The same uniformly distributed load per metre.
  • Simply supported ends.

Only the span changes.

The increase from 2 metres to 3 metres is 50%.

But the increase in structural demand is much greater.

Bending Demand Increases by 125%

For this simplified beam model, maximum bending moment increases with the square of the span.

(3 ÷ 2)² = 2.25

The 3-metre span therefore experiences 2.25 times the bending moment of the 2-metre span under the same load per metre.

The profile has not become stronger.

The demand on it has increased.

Deflection Becomes About 5 Times Greater

For the same model, elastic bending deflection increases with the fourth power of the span.

(3 ÷ 2)⁴ ≈ 5.06

That means approximately 5 times the deflection, despite using the same steel section.

This comparison illustrates why support spacing matters. Actual solar purlins may have continuous spans, concentrated module loads, different restraints and other behaviour that requires a project-specific analysis.

What Happens When We Reduce the Number of Supports?

Fewer supports may reduce component quantities and installation work.

But increasing their spacing can also increase:

  • Bending demand on purlins or rafters.
  • Member deflection.
  • Forces transferred through individual support connections.
  • Reactions carried by the remaining supports and anchors.

Reducing supports is an engineering decision. The revised arrangement needs verification.

More Thickness Is Only One Possible Response

An engineer may address a longer span through:

  • A deeper or differently shaped section.
  • Increased thickness.
  • Additional supports.
  • Revised member orientation or restraint.
  • Changes to the overall structural arrangement.

The appropriate choice depends on strength, stability, deflection, connections and installation requirements.

What Should EPC Companies Check?

Before comparing mounting-structure quotations, ask:

What support spacing was used in the structural calculations?

Then confirm that the installation drawings and actual site arrangement match that spacing.

A report prepared for a 2-metre span does not establish suitability for a 3-metre span.

Likewise, relocating supports during installation should trigger a review of the affected members and connections.

The profile specification and the support layout must be evaluated together.

Same profile. Same thickness. Longer span. Greater structural demand.

When evaluating a solar mounting structure, check both the steel section and the distance between its supports.
